# Rate Limits v0.3 Product Specification

- **Status:** Integrated release-candidate scope
- **Date:** 2026-09-27
- **Release baseline:** `origin/main` / v0.2.1 merge at `8fe0fdc`
- **Implementation constraint:** frozen v0.3 scope for release-candidate integration.

## Evidence boundaries

This specification uses four evidence sets:

- `docs/market-benchmark.md` for market facts and the RADAR dispositions reproduced in section 9.
- `feature/v0.3-ui-hardening` (`e8d1b5f`, `b7e5e50`) for the completed Graphite / Glass / OLED and reset-countdown experiment. This supersedes the identical older `feature/themes-reset-countdown` commits.
- `feature/quota-history` (`dcc83e1`) for the completed, bounded 24-hour local store and its tests.
- `feature/quota-prediction-engine` (`c70d645`) for the independently tested deterministic prediction prototype and its 32 tests.
- `origin/main` (`8fe0fdc`) for the released product baseline: tray and provider behavior plus structured failures, last-good retention, conservative Antigravity freshness, and Windows CI.

`feature/quota-history` contributes an unwired but complete local storage library. Release-candidate integration connects successful real-provider observations to that store and feeds the deterministic prediction engine from retained current-cycle samples. History and prediction remain optional infrastructure: their failures never block provider refresh or tray behavior.

## 1 - PRODUCT GOAL

v0.3 should make Rate Limits more useful at the moment a user asks, "Can I keep working, and when will my quota be available again?" It should add reset timing and a cautious consumption trajectory while making the age and trustworthiness of every reading obvious.

The product remains a compact Windows tray utility. Its primary surface is still a small dashboard of normalized provider quota windows, opened from the tray. v0.3 improves three things:

1. **Actionability:** show when each quota window resets and whether current usage is likely to last until then.
2. **Trust:** distinguish live data, passive cache data, stale readings, and failed refreshes without discarding useful last-good values.
3. **Continuity:** keep a short local observation history so a restart does not erase the basis for a projection.

v0.3 must not become a full analytics platform, billing dashboard, observability suite, or generic AI management framework. It does not compare plans, calculate spend, manage agents, synchronize accounts, or provide long-range reporting.

## 2 - IN SCOPE

### Reset countdown

**Decision:** In scope.

**User value:** A percentage alone does not tell a user when capacity returns. A countdown turns the provider's reset timestamp into an actionable wait time while preserving the exact reset time.

**Supporting source/experiment:** `feature/themes-reset-countdown` implemented locally ticking countdowns, compact relative durations, and absolute reset times. Its UX-hardening follow-up covers expired and distant dates. The market benchmark disposes reset countdown as **ADOPT**.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Every window with a valid future `resetAt` shows both the absolute local reset time and a compact countdown such as `in 2h 17m`.
- The countdown advances from the local clock and performs no provider fetch.
- A reset less than one minute away reads `<1m`, never `0m`.
- An expired timestamp reads `Reset time passed`; it never displays a negative countdown.
- A missing or invalid timestamp omits the reset line and invents no fallback time.

### Graphite / Glass / OLED

**Decision:** In scope as three frontend themes. Graphite is the default.

**User value:** Graphite provides the stable everyday surface, OLED reduces glow and light output for dark displays, and Glass offers an optional depth treatment without changing the information model. Theme choice accommodates display conditions and personal preference while keeping all states readable.

**Supporting source/experiment:** `feature/themes-reset-countdown` implemented tokenized Graphite, Glass, and OLED styles, persistence, focus states, reduced-transparency fallback, and screenshot coverage. `docs/glass-native-transparency.md` proves only an in-window frontend treatment and defers native shell transparency.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Graphite is used on first launch and remains the fallback for invalid stored settings.
- The selected theme persists across restarts and applies without changing provider data or layout semantics.
- All three themes preserve text contrast, progress-value readability, focus visibility, and status-color meaning.
- Glass is CSS-only over the app's own opaque window/background. It has an opaque fallback when backdrop blur is unavailable or reduced transparency is requested.
- No theme changes the Tauri window to transparent or applies native Mica/Acrylic effects.

### Quota history

**Decision:** In scope as a minimal local observation store that enables prediction. Historical charts and analytics are out of scope.

**User value:** A short, persisted series lets projections continue across app restarts and gives the prediction a real reset-cycle basis. Users see only the provenance needed to judge a projection, not a reporting workspace.

**Supporting source/experiment:** `feature/quota-history` provides normalized persisted observations, and the deterministic prediction prototype consumes that exact shape. The completed experiment finding is that prediction is useful primarily with persisted history.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- A successful normalized sample can be recorded as `{providerId, windowLabel, usedPercent, observedAt, resetAt?}`.
- History is retained for 24 hours and bounded to the newest 500 observations per provider/window; older rows are discarded first.
- Identical observations at the same timestamp collapse to one entry instead of growing the store on repeated cache reads.
- History survives an application restart and machine sleep/resume.
- Credentials, tokens, account identifiers, prompts, raw provider responses, and cost data are never stored.
- Mock providers are excluded from history and prediction.

### Projection

**Decision:** In scope as a confidence-gated, deterministic estimate. This is the benchmark's **TEST** pattern promoted only to a bounded v0.3 product experiment, not a general forecasting feature.

**User value:** At sufficient confidence, the user can see whether current consumption is likely to fit before reset instead of repeatedly doing the arithmetic manually.

**Supporting source/experiment:** `feature/quota-prediction-engine` provides deterministic least-squares burn rate, reset-cycle segmentation, projection to reset, estimated exhaustion, and diagnostic basis data. The experiment found a six-hour analysis window appropriate for the tested short-window behavior and explicitly rejected machine learning.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Projection uses only the newest detected reset-cycle segment for the matching provider and window.
- When eligible, the UI shows projected percentage at reset and burn rate together.
- `Likely exhaustion` appears only when the engine estimates exhaustion before the future reset.
- Values are clamped and rounded for display without changing stored observations.
- The same inputs and reference time produce the same output; the engine does not read the wall clock internally.

### Confidence

**Decision:** In scope as both the prediction visibility gate and a visible supporting label.

**User value:** Confidence prevents a thin or stale sample series from presenting a precise-looking forecast as fact.

**Supporting source/experiment:** The prediction prototype defines the deterministic ladder `insufficient`, `low`, `medium`, and `high`, based on reset bound, source freshness, sample count, span, and sampling density. Its tests cover every level and sparse/stale cases. The experiment finding is not to surface low-confidence risk alone.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Prediction content is visible only at `medium` or `high` confidence.
- The visible confidence label is exactly `Medium` or `High` and appears with its supporting projection values.
- `low` and `insufficient` results produce no prediction block and no standalone risk badge.
- Confidence thresholds are deterministic and not user-configurable in v0.3.
- A prediction detail exposes basis such as `Based on 8 samples over 3h; current reset cycle only` without exposing stored history as a chart.

### Stale awareness

**Decision:** In scope across all provider cards and the global refresh summary.

**User value:** A quota number is only actionable if the user knows whether it is live, cached, old, or retained after failure. Keeping the last-good value prevents blank cards while explicit provenance prevents that value from masquerading as current.

**Supporting source/experiment:** v0.2.1 retains last-good windows after failed refreshes and makes unknown Antigravity freshness conservative. The prediction prototype caps stale-source confidence at `low`. The market benchmark disposes stale-aware quota and freshness badges as **ADOPT**.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Every visible reading has an understandable origin and age.
- Cached data never presents as live, and unknown source age presents as stale.
- A failed refresh preserves the previous windows and reports the failure separately from data freshness.
- Stale data cannot produce a visible v0.3 prediction because its confidence is capped at `low`.
- Status language follows section 6 in the dashboard, tooltips, and accessibility labels.

## 4 - PREDICTION UX

Prediction is a compact block inside an individual quota-window row. It is not a provider-level badge, dashboard banner, or separate page.

### Visibility rule

Show the prediction block only when all of the following are true:

- confidence is `medium` or `high`;
- the newest observation is not already at 100%; and
- the prediction has a usable future reset timestamp.

The default visible block is:

```text
Projection
Projected at reset   78%
Likely exhaustion    Sat 14:20     (only when exhaustion is estimated before reset)
Burn rate            3.2%/h
Confidence           Medium
```

`Projected at reset`, `Burn rate`, and `Confidence` are a single supporting group. `Likely exhaustion` is shown only when `willExhaustBeforeReset` is true and carries its local date/time. The internal risk band is never shown as a standalone `Low`, `Medium`, or `High` badge. No warning icon or color appears without at least the projection or exhaustion value and confidence that supports it.

Hovering or keyboard-focusing the projection group exposes a short basis tooltip, for example: `Based on 8 samples over 3h; current reset cycle only`. This is provenance, not a history browser.

### Behavior by condition

| Condition | Product behavior |
|---|---|
| Insufficient history (`insufficient`) | Hide the prediction block and all risk language. Keep usage, reset, and freshness content unchanged. Do not show a numeric placeholder. |
| Low confidence (`low`) | Hide the prediction block and all risk language. Do not surface `risk` alone. |
| Stale data | The engine caps confidence at `low`, so hide the prediction block. The stale label and source age remain visible. |
| Refresh failed but last-good retained | Do not compute from the failed attempt. If retained history still yields `medium`/`high` freshness-compliant confidence, prediction may remain with `Confidence` and basis visible; otherwise hide it. The refresh failure is always shown separately. |
| Reset timestamp missing | Hide projected-at-reset and likely-exhaustion content. Because confidence is capped at `low`, the entire prediction block is hidden in v0.3. |
| Reset timestamp expired | Hide the prediction block and show `Reset time passed` on the reset line. Do not project against an expired bound. |
| Limit already 100% | Replace the prediction block with `Limit reached`. Keep the reset countdown and freshness state visible. Do not describe an observed 100% as a forecast. |
| Medium/high confidence but exhaustion after reset | Show projected-at-reset, burn rate, and confidence; omit `Likely exhaustion`. |

## 5 - HISTORY UX / PRIVACY

### User visibility

v0.3 does not ship a history page, chart, heatmap, export, or table. History is user-visible only as:

- the prediction basis tooltip described in section 4;
- a Settings note stating `Local prediction history: 24 hours on this device`; and
- a `Clear history` action in Settings.

This keeps history accountable and removable without turning the app into an analytics surface.

### Local-only expectations

- Store only normalized quota observations needed by the prediction engine.
- Use local application storage on the device, under the same WebView2 user-data boundary as existing settings (`rate-limits.quota-history.v1`). This is an implementation choice consistent with v0.2.1 and adds no cloud service or credential command.
- Never store credentials, tokens, account identity, prompts, raw responses, model names, prices, or cost estimates.
- No cloud sync, account, telemetry, remote logging, or background upload is part of v0.3.
- Clearing WebView application data also clears history, matching the existing settings behavior.

### Retention and size

- Retain observations for **24 hours** from `observedAt`.
- Collapse identical observations at the same timestamp rather than appending duplicates.
- Keep at most the newest 500 observations per provider/window. Prune expired rows first, then oldest rows if a bound is still exceeded.
- Never retain a sample rejected by prediction ingest validation, including unparseable timestamps or blank window labels.

### Clear history

`Clear history` is included because the bounded store exposes a clean local clear operation. It immediately removes all stored observations, clears derived in-memory predictions, and disables prediction until new eligible samples accumulate. It does not alter theme, refresh, or startup settings and does not require an account.

### Persistence failure

History persistence is best-effort. A read, parse, quota, or write failure must not block provider refresh, blank a provider card, or surface as a provider error. The app continues showing live/cached quota and reset data, history-backed prediction disappears or remains based only on valid in-memory samples, and Settings may show one neutral note: `Prediction history is unavailable on this device.`

## 6 - STALE SEMANTICS

Freshness and request outcome are separate axes. "Stale" describes displayed data age; "refresh failed" describes the latest attempt. Neither is a synonym for the other, and neither replaces the structured provider error.

### Terms and exact presentation

| Term | Meaning | Default presentation | Detail text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provider live | The latest refresh successfully fetched this provider directly from its service and the displayed data is within the app freshness threshold. | `Live` | `Checked 14:32` |
| Cached | Data comes from a passive local source, such as the Antigravity quota cache, and the source timestamp is within that provider's freshness threshold. | `Cached` | `Source updated 12m ago` |
| Stale | Displayed data is older than its allowed freshness window, or its source timestamp is missing/invalid. Stale may apply to live-derived or cached data. | `Stale` | `Showing data from 08:10` or `Source update time unknown` |
| Refresh failed but last-good retained | The latest refresh attempt failed and the previous successful windows remain visible. This is a request outcome, not a freshness verdict. | `Refresh failed` | `Showing last good data from 08:10` plus the structured failure message |

Rules:

- `Live` is never used for passive cache reads.
- `Cached` is never used for a failed live request.
- `Stale` is a qualifier about age and can coexist with `Refresh failed`: `Refresh failed - stale data from 08:10`.
- An unknown timestamp is stale, never fresh or live.
- `Refresh failed` keeps last-good windows visible and keeps `checkedAt`, `sourceUpdatedAt`, and the structured error distinct.
- The global footer uses `Refresh overdue` for failure to complete a refresh cycle. It must not call that condition `stale` unless the displayed data itself has crossed the freshness threshold.
- Tooltips and screen-reader labels use the same terms as the visible status.

## 7 - VISUAL SYSTEM

### Graphite - default

Graphite is the neutral, low-glare dark theme and the first-paint fallback. It uses solid elevated cards, restrained borders, tabular numeric values, and the existing calm/warn/critical semantic accents. It is the compatibility reference for spacing, hierarchy, and contrast.

### Glass - optional frontend treatment

Glass uses translucent card surfaces, restrained backdrop blur, and an in-window gradient depth layer. Text, borders, controls, and status semantics remain the same as Graphite. The window itself remains opaque.

Glass must:

- fall back to solid cards when `backdrop-filter` is unavailable;
- fall back to solid cards when the user requests reduced transparency;
- remain readable over every underlying surface; and
- avoid making blur, glow, or translucency necessary to understand state.

### OLED

OLED uses a true-black canvas and near-black solid cards with minimal borders and no status glow. Semantic status colors remain distinct and meet the same contrast expectations as Graphite. It is a display-comfort theme, not a separate information layout.

### Native transparency

Native Mica/Acrylic and a transparent Tauri window are deferred. v0.3 ships no `transparent` window configuration or native window effects. The separate experiment required in section 3 must prove startup, resize/drag, tray show/hide, close-to-tray, single-instance focus, autostart-hidden launch, Windows 10/11 behavior, and WebView2 fallback before native transparency can be reconsidered.

## 8 - ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

These product-level criteria are the v0.3 release gate.

1. **Countdown without fetch:** advancing the local clock changes every active countdown and reset formatting without invoking a provider command or changing `checkedAt`.
2. **Reset edge cases:** a future timestamp shows absolute and relative time, `<1m` truncates safely, an expired timestamp reads `Reset time passed`, and missing/invalid reset data produces no reset line.
3. **Theme persistence:** Graphite is the first-run theme; Graphite, Glass, and OLED each persist across restart and preserve identical content hierarchy.
4. **Glass fallback:** disabling backdrop blur or requesting reduced transparency produces an opaque, readable Glass fallback without layout loss.
5. **Native transparency absent:** installed-window configuration remains opaque and contains no native Mica/Acrylic effect.
6. **History survives restart:** an eligible observation written before restart is available to prediction after restart within the 24-hour retention window.
7. **History retention:** rows older than 24 hours are removed, identical timestamped observations collapse, and the newest-500-per-window bound cannot grow without limit.
8. **History privacy:** stored history contains only normalized observation fields and no secret, account identity, prompt, raw response, model, price, or cost data.
9. **Clear history:** clearing removes all persisted observations and derived predictions immediately; normal quota refresh continues to work.
10. **Reset-cycle isolation:** prediction never uses samples from a prior reset cycle, including after a percentage drop greater than five points or a forward `resetAt` change.
11. **Projection determinism:** identical observations, target window, and reference time produce identical prediction output across runs.
12. **Confidence gate:** `medium` and `high` predictions show projected-at-reset, burn rate, and confidence together; `low` and `insufficient` show no prediction block and no standalone risk badge.
13. **Exhaustion precision:** `Likely exhaustion` is present only when estimated exhaustion occurs before the usable reset timestamp.
14. **Limit reached:** a window observed at 100% shows `Limit reached`, not a forecast, while reset and freshness information remain visible.
15. **No stale-as-live:** cached, old, or timestamp-indeterminate data never presents as `Live`; stale data never produces a visible prediction.
16. **Failure retention:** after a failed refresh, the card keeps its last-good windows, shows `Refresh failed`, exposes the structured failure separately, and does not relabel the failure as stale unless the retained data is old.
17. **Persistence resilience:** forcing history reads or writes to fail leaves provider cards usable and never turns the storage failure into a provider error.
18. **Mock exclusion:** Claude and Grok mock data is never recorded in history and never receives a projection.
19. **Accessibility:** countdowns and status changes are exposed to assistive technology; keyboard focus is visible in all themes; color is not the only status cue.
20. **Windows CI:** a clean v0.3 installer passes the Windows CI job with locked Rust tests, frontend tests, typecheck/bundle, Tauri build, and NSIS installer artifact creation.

## 10 - RELEASE PLAN

### Phase 1 - History

Integration order:

1. Define the normalized observation schema and validation rules.
2. Add the 24-hour local store with duplicate collapse and a newest-500-per-window bound.
3. Add best-effort load/save behavior and persistence-failure degradation.
4. Add the Settings retention note and `Clear history` action.

Exit criteria: acceptance items 6-9 and 17 pass; live quota behavior is unchanged when history is empty, corrupted, cleared, or unavailable.

### Phase 2 - Themes / countdown

Integration order:

1. Integrate the theme token system and persisted Graphite / Glass / OLED selector with Graphite as default.
2. Integrate local reset countdown and expired/invalid timestamp formatting.
3. Apply the accessibility-hardened focus, status-label, and reduced-transparency behavior from the experiment.
4. Keep the Tauri window opaque and run visual checks in all three themes.

Exit criteria: acceptance items 1-5 and 19 pass without provider or storage dependencies.

### Phase 3 - Prediction wiring

Integration order:

1. Feed validated current-cycle history from phase 1 into the deterministic engine from `feature/quota-prediction-engine`.
2. Apply the medium/high visibility predicate at the individual window level.
3. Render projected-at-reset, optional likely-exhaustion, burn rate, confidence, and basis as one supporting group.
4. Apply the insufficient, low, stale, missing-reset, expired-reset, and 100% behaviors from section 4.

Exit criteria: acceptance items 10-15 and 18 pass; no standalone risk badge or low-confidence number is reachable in the UI.

### Phase 4 - Integration review / CI

Integration order:

1. Reconcile provider status language across live, cached, stale, and refresh-failed states.
2. Verify structured errors remain separate from freshness and retain last-good windows.
3. Run provider contract tests, prediction tests, history tests, theme/countdown tests, typecheck, and build.
4. Produce the Windows NSIS installer through CI and execute the installed-app smoke path.

Exit criteria: all section 8 criteria pass on the integrated branch and a v0.3 Windows installer artifact is produced. The unsigned-installer/SmartScreen signing policy is recorded in release notes rather than silently changed.
